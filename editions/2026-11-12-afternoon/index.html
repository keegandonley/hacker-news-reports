Jensen Huang has resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, the board says, effective immediately

CFO Colette Kress takes over as interim chief while the company's retained investigators probe Huang's affair with a woman U.S. intelligence suspects spied for China

From the AI Desk★ 2488 pts · 2410 comments

SANTA CLARA, CALIFORNIA — Nvidia's board of directors announced Thursday that Jensen Huang, 63, has resigned as president, chief executive officer and member of the board, effective immediately, ending a 33-year run as the only CEO of the company he co-founded in 1993.[1] Colette Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, becomes interim CEO while keeping the CFO title; the board has opened a permanent-successor search covering internal and external candidates.[1] The decision came amid an investigation into Huang's affair with Alice Huang (no relation), a Singapore-based Megaspeed International executive whom U.S. intelligence agencies suspect was spying for China; the board had been preparing to oust him, two sources confirmed to CBS News on Wednesday night.[1] The Associated Press article behind the thread's link met this paper's bot wall; the account above is sourced from CBS News and CNET reporting and the announcement quoted in the thread.[1][2][3]

The reporting around the resignation is layered. The New York Times reported Wednesday that five employees alleged Huang was also romantically involved with a subordinate, vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell; Reuters, after the announcement, reported a second employee relationship; and The Information tied Alice Huang to a network that facilitated $7.5 billion in chip sales to China, with U.S. investigators saying she gained access to 240,000 export-banned chips. Megaspeed is under investigation in the U.S. and Singapore for allegedly smuggling them into China. The board has retained Joon H. Kim, a former acting U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York, and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to run an independent investigation; a proposed class action was filed Monday.[1][2]

Nvidia shed almost $500 billion in value as the news broke and closed Thursday down 4.3 percent at $166.19, down from above $240 in October.[1] Sen. Elizabeth Warren said the departure "doesn't absolve" the company: "NVIDIA executives and NVIDIA's board of directors still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls."[1] The thread — at 2,410 comments the most-discussed of the day — argued over who arranged the exit and what the chip-smuggling investigation will find; this morning's paper reported the ouster preparation, and the afternoon thread carries the resignation itself.[3]

"The sudden resignation of Jensen Huang is about more than succession at the world's most important AI chipmaker. Nvidia's leadership crisis is a stress test for the AI boom."— Stephanie Balaouras, Forrester, to CNET[2]
AI Desk · Markets

Anthropic publishes its IPO roadshow, with $17 billion of quarterly revenue in the frame

From the AI Desk★ 735 pts · 745 cmts

Anthropic has put up its initial-public-offering roadshow page, presenting Q3 2026 revenue of $17.0 billion — up more than 12 times year over year — with more than 11,900 customers at or above $100,000 in trailing-twelve-month revenue, a more-than-fourfold compute-efficiency gain since Q1 2025, and a 15 percent adjusted operating margin.[4] The headline net loss of $23.3 billion carries a footnote: $21.9 billion of it is a non-cash charge from the increase in the fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs as the company's own valuation rose.[4]

The page leans on named customers — Bristol Myers Squibb, Accenture, AIG — with figures like 50,000-plus employees holding Claude access and one legal-software firm recapturing 4.3 non-billable hours per lawyer weekly across a 31-firm study.[4] The company frames the offering as a public benefit corporation "dedicated to securing [AI's] benefits and mitigating its risks."[4]

The thread's top comment was one word long — "enshittification" — and the replies took it seriously, trading local-LLM setups as the hedge against a public-company Anthropic; one reader's answer was simply a capable laptop, llama.cpp or Ollama, and a harness.[5]

AI Desk · Models

Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a fast execution model priced under its own frontier tier

From the AI Desk★ 385 pts · 251 cmts

Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November, an execution model for "everyday coding and agentic workflows" that sits below the frontier Carbon and Argon tiers but beats Argon on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default thinking level — 51 to 50, reaching 54 at a new xhigh setting.[7] Pricing is flat across the entire one-million-token context window: $1.50 per million input tokens, $7.50 per million output.[7]

Against Gemini 3.8 Flash, Google says Hydrogen finishes tasks 2.5 times faster at the high default setting, more than triples Terminal-Bench 4.1 scores (57 percent versus 18.2), and cuts fabricated claims by more than five times; it ranks fifth on Agent Arena and leads its class on HLE-Diamond at 58.8 percent.[7] It rolls out today across Search for all users, the Gemini app, AI Studio, Sheets, and Antigravity for paid subscribers, with four thinking levels from low to xhigh.[7]

The thread argued about which tier actually does the world's work — one reader called this class "the workhorses" of everyday use, another countered that frontier-tier models on a subscription get more done, and a third noted enterprises past 150 seats are still on API pricing.[8]

Jane Street's Socratic-tutor skill keeps climbing, and the thread turns on its novelty

Language Desk★ 635 pts · ▲ +223 since the last edition

Jane Street's socratic-tutor agent skill, covered in this morning's edition at 412 points, has grown 54 percent to 635 — one day after landing on the repository's front page. The skill itself is unchanged: a roughly 100-line prompt that diagnoses a learner with 3–5 questions, tracks subskills at levels 0–5, and teaches "primarily by asking questions."[6]

What changed is the argument underneath it. The rest of the thread split on why the repository carries 6.3 thousand stars and 434 forks at all. Language Desk · Essay

A formal-methods essay works through why correct software resists being proved correct

Language Desk★ 336 pts · 150 cmts

An extended talk transcript, "Why Writing Correct Software Is Hard — and why math (alone) won't help us," walks from Hilbert's program through Gödel, Church and Turing to a quantified claim: the function tying a program's size to the difficulty of proving anything about it outgrows every computable function. The Busy Beaver bound for six-state machines already exceeds 7.4 × 1036534, and a 1,919-state Turing machine exists whose termination is independent of ZFC — a program comparable to 100–200 lines of Python that mathematics cannot settle even in principle.[9]

The essay's second half moves from impossibility to cost: verification is not just undecidable in general but infeasibly expensive in the finite cases that matter, which is why "CS is not math" — mathematics studies the small circle of fully provable objects, and computer science largely lives outside it.[9]

The thread's best-remembered line was borrowed, not new: "a computer scientist has as much to do with computers as an astronomer has to do with telescopes" — which astronomers in the thread promptly disputed. More substantively, readers welcomed a treatment of verification that does not assume AI will simply make proving easy: building programs that can be verified at all remains a hard design problem.[10]

Tools Desk · Linux

bootvine replaces the boot log's wall of text with a live tree of systemd jobs

Tools Desk★ 336 pts · 129 cmts

bootvine, a new Rust tool (85 percent of its codebase) published on Codeberg, draws the units still pending during boot as a live tree on the console — in the spirit of nix-output-monitor — and uses the space the tree leaves for a timeline of the slowest units that already ran. It replaces scrolling kernel and systemd lines, follows shutdowns, answers LUKS passphrase prompts in place, and shows why a unit failed with its last journal lines.[11]

It needs systemd 246 or newer as PID 1 and a systemd initrd to show the full boot; a Nix flake boots a sample NixOS machine with slow and failing units in QEMU without touching the host. The guide documents the screen, per-distribution installs, and the architecture. License: GPL-3.0-or-later.[11]

The thread's Gentoo wing asked what the OpenRC equivalent looks like and found the answer reflective: on minimal systems the kernel boot is already faster than the BIOS work that precedes it. One longtime systemd opponent conceded the point of the exercise: "I hate systemd with a burning passion, but this is the first time I ever thought it might be slightly redeemed."[12]

Microsoft 365 runs a "service degradation" as Exchange Online mailboxes go unreachable

Cloud Desk★ 285 pts · 311 cmts

Microsoft is investigating reports of users unable to access Exchange Online mailboxes "through multiple connection methods in the Americas," tracked as EX1550543 with a "Service Degradation" banner on Microsoft 365 for business and enterprise. The 7:30 p.m. UTC update, quoted in the thread: "We're aggressively pursuing all pathways to reduce impact. As a precaution, we've paused all deployments and configuration changes within the affected environment."[13][14]

The status page itself did not inspire confidence: readers reported it loading very slowly and, at times, returning a 502 from the Azure Front Door service in front of it — so the primary public signal of the outage was itself intermittently down.[14]

The thread re-ran the on-premises argument with numbers attached. One operator wrote that the largest site he works with, which moved to M365 hybrid last year, "has incurred more Exchange downtime today than they had all of last year with their on-prem Exchange servers"; the rejoinder, from another reader, was that redundancy inside the service was never the same as redundancy of the service — "from the customer standpoint, the service as a whole is a single point of failure: it either works or it doesn't."[14]

Practical Tech Corner
Field · On-Device AI

smolmaxxing runs 16-billion-parameter models on a TV box with one gigabyte of RAM

Practical Tech Corner★ 229 pts · 80 cmts

The smolmaxxing project publishes llama.cpp patches that run 8B and 16B mixture-of-experts models on devices with about one gigabyte of usable memory — so far a Xiaomi TV box and a jailbroken first-generation iPad Air on iOS 12 — by keeping only part of the model in RAM and streaming the rest from flash. "Every number in this repo was measured on the device itself," the README states.[15]

The mechanisms are concrete: a 1-bit lookup-table kernel written for the Cortex-A55 and repacked 2-bit and ternary formats for the A7, which lacks dot-product instructions; output-vocabulary rows dropped for words a small model rarely emits; hot experts kept in RAM while cold ones are paged from flash with the next layer prefetched; and, on the TV, a headless mode that stops the launcher and Google services while a model runs.[15]

The author, posting to the thread as the project's developer, said phones, single-board computers and routers are next, and accepted a reader's suggestion for the hardest target on offer — a vape with a screen on it.[16]

The Culture Desk
Culture · Legal

Nintendo's DMCA notices remove 144 repositories of Switch mod-chip firmware from GitHub

Culture Desk★ 226 pts · 151 cmts

Four DMCA takedown notices from Nintendo, including one dated 6 November now posted by GitHub, targeted two Switch emulators — Yuzu and Citron Neo — and the firmware for mod chips such as Picofly, which can be soldered to a Switch mainboard to circumvent copy protection. The firmware's 144 repositories were removed in a single sweep; Nintendo's notice says the Switch's technological protection measures "permit the Nintendo Switch console to interact only with legitimate Nintendo video game files."[17]

The sweep arrives days after emulators ran the remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time on PC, and TorrentFreak reports copies of the firmware remain hosted elsewhere on GitHub — in 2024, a single Nintendo takedown removed 8,500 copies of Yuzu across 12 mirrors.[17]

The thread's practical question was where such projects can live at all: self-hosted GitLab or Forgejo instances, providers in jurisdictions where emulators are legal, or a Radicle node over Tor. One reader's legal question went unanswered: backing up a game he owns and running it in an emulator with firmware extracted from his own device — "what law am I breaking?"[18]

Culture · Games

The unreleased Quake 4 expansion is playable at last, via openQ4's Awakening Edition

Culture Desk★ 214 pts · 76 cmts

openQ4 0.14.1, a free open-source engine and game-code replacement for Quake 4, ships the expansion that Raven Software and Ritual Entertainment built but never released: all 13 maps of The Awakening run on openQ4's own code, with the alpha's unfinished pieces — the Valkaryne finale, the beach death-ray turret, the Pain Lord's weak spot — completed where the original content stopped working as designed. Justin Marshall recovered and published the material; openQ4 itself ships none of it, requiring the user's own files.[19]

The release also brings stable shadow mapping, Vulkan on Windows as the default for new installs, smarter bots that take orders, and experimental Android and OpenXR VR builds — the latter qualified on a single phone and no headset hardware, the project warns. The thread, meanwhile, argued over how much of the write-up was machine-written: "finished by AI," one reader said of the description, to which another replied that a tool with no agency deserves no more credit than a hammer.[20]

Corrections & Clarifications

"Surpasses Argon." Google's post says Hydrogen beats Argon on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at default settings; the cited margin is 51 to 50, and the higher 54 arrives only at the new xhigh tier. Hydrogen's Agent Arena rank is fifth, which the post also states.

A $23.3 billion loss, mostly on paper. The IPO page's headline net loss includes a $21.9 billion non-cash charge triggered by Anthropic's own rising valuation; the operating figure Anthropic highlights is a 15 percent adjusted operating margin. The loss is real accounting, but it is not cash burn.

"Massive" sweep. Of the takedowns IGN reports, the 144 removed repositories were all one project — mod-chip firmware. The two emulators named include Yuzu, which Nintendo effectively took down in 2024; TorrentFreak finds the firmware still hosted elsewhere on GitHub.

"Finished by modders." openQ4's Awakening runs an unfinished alpha recovered by a collector, with openQ4's own code filling in what never worked — the project says it ships none of the expansion's content. The Android and VR builds are qualified on one phone and no headset respectively.
